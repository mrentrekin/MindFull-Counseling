After the
Affair

A five-module, self-paced recovery course for the weeks and months after discovery — stabilizing the crisis, understanding how it happened, learning to talk again, rebuilding trust on evidence, and deciding what comes next on purpose rather than in panic.

24 lessons · 27 tools · Free · Both partners

Discovery collapses time. Everything feels like it has to be decided tonight — whether to stay, what to tell people, whether anything before this was real. Almost none of it does.

This course is built to slow that down. It doesn't assume you'll stay together and it doesn't assume you'll separate. It assumes that whichever you choose, you'd rather choose it clearly than be swept into it.

01

Stop the Bleeding

Contain the crisis, set boundaries that hold, and learn what flooding is doing to both of you.

02

Understand What Happened

Build an accountable explanation — responsibility first, context second, blame never transferred.

03

Learning to Talk Again

A structure for hard conversations: the container, the questions worth asking, and repair after escalation.

04

Rebuilding Trust

Replace reassurance with evidence, and give transparency an exit rather than a permanent role.

05

Choosing Your Future

Assess readiness, separate forgiveness from reconciliation, and put the next ninety days in writing.

This will help if

  • Discovery was recent and the days feel unmanageable
  • You keep having the same conversation and it keeps ending badly
  • You want structure between therapy sessions
  • You don't know yet what you want, and want to decide well

This is not

  • Therapy, or a substitute for it
  • A diagnosis or an assessment of your relationship
  • A push toward staying, or toward leaving
  • Appropriate where there is violence or coercive control

If you're in danger right now

Where there is violence, coercion or controlling behavior, stalking, threats, or thoughts of suicide, this course is not the right first step. In the U.S.: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988); National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788.
